Frequently asked questions

Will it leave my furniture wet?

It leaves the surface slightly damp, not wet. Because the mist is so fine, you are putting down a fraction of the liquid a trigger bottle would over the same area, and it dries in minutes. Hold it about 30cm back and keep the gun moving rather than resting on one spot.

Can I use my own cleaner in it?

Yes, and that is the point of an open reservoir. Any thin water-based liquid works: enzyme cleaners, diluted white vinegar, plain water. Avoid bleach, anything oil-based, essential oils undiluted, and anything thick enough to clog a nozzle.

Is it safe to spray around my pet?

Treat the room with the pet out of it and let the surfaces dry before they come back. Do not spray the gun onto an animal. Safety depends entirely on what you put in the reservoir, so follow the label of whatever solution you are using.

Does it disinfect or kill bacteria?

No. It is a sprayer, not a disinfectant. It atomises whatever liquid you put in it. Any claim about what that liquid does belongs to the liquid's own label, not to this device.

Will it work on carpet and rugs?

Yes. Carpet is one of the surfaces that benefits most, because the residue sits down in the pile where wiping never reaches and the pad underneath holds it. Do a light even pass rather than a heavy one.

Will it stain or discolour fabric?

The gun does not stain anything; it only delivers liquid. Whether a given solution is safe on a given fabric depends on both. Spot-test an out-of-sight area first on anything delicate, unlined, or dry-clean-only.

How is this different from a $12 spray bottle?

Coverage and droplet size. A trigger bottle puts heavy droplets on a small patch, which is fine for a stain and useless for a room. This puts a fine mist over a whole area with less liquid, which is what a spread-out smell actually needs. You can keep using the same solution.
