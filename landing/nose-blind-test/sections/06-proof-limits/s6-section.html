Do not take our word for it. Take five minutes.

We should say this plainly: we are a new brand. We have not earned your trust yet, and a number on a website is not how trust gets earned anyway.

So here are three tests you can run yourself, the day it arrives, before you have committed to anything.

  1. The stopwatch test. Start a timer. Cover the entire couch, seat backs and arms included. Compare that against the last time you tried it with a trigger bottle.
  2. The tissue test. Press a dry tissue flat against the cushion right after the pass, then again a few minutes later. This is the one that tells you whether "fine mist" means anything.
  3. The guest test. You cannot judge your own house. Ask someone who does not live there, a week later, without telling them why. Their face answers the question.

If any of the three disappoints you, that is exactly what the thirty days are for.

What it is not

You have been sold this category before, so here is the short list of things we are not going to tell you.

  • It is not a disinfectant. We make no claim that it kills bacteria, viruses, germs or dust mites. Any product making that claim is legally required to be registered with the EPA first. Read this category carefully and notice how many are not.
  • It is not a fragrance. If you want the living room to smell like a candle, buy a candle.
  • It is not a carpet extractor. Urine soaked through to the underlay needs liquid poured on it. A mist will not carry enough volume down there.
  • It is not a grooming spray. Never mist it onto an animal. It treats surfaces.
  • It does not lock you in. Fill it with the water-based enzyme cleaner you already trust. You are not buying a refill subscription.

If your dog or cat has suddenly started having accidents indoors, see your vet. New behavior can signal a UTI or another medical issue. This handles what is already in the carpet. It does not replace a vet visit.
