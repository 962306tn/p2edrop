# Image briefs

Ten images to generate, plus one graphic that should not be generated at all.
Filenames are what `index.html` and `velahush.js` already reference — drop a file
in with the matching name and it takes over from the placeholder automatically.

## Read this first — it applies to every shot

**Every image must survive being printed in black and white.** The Modernist skin
forces `filter: grayscale(1)` on all photography; the Cupertino skin shows it in
colour. So the composition has to work on *tonal separation*, not hue. A cream dog
on a cream rug, white mist on a white sofa, or a white product against a white
wall will all collapse into mush in one of the two skins. Give every subject a
background at least two stops away from it.

**Lock the product before generating anything else.** Generate one clean render of
the unit first, from the real supplier photo, then feed that render back as a
reference image for every other shot. Without it the gun changes shape in every
frame and the page falls apart.

The unit is a **cordless handheld dry-mist sprayer**: a compact white/light-grey
body with an inverted-U carry handle on top, a transparent cylindrical reservoir
bottle mounted below the handle, and a circular atomiser nozzle at the front.
Roughly the size of a cordless drill, held one-handed.

> **The supplier photo carries "K12 Blue Ray Sprayer" branding** on both the unit
> and its box, legible at hero size. Any generated image must show the device
> **completely unbranded** — no text, no logos, no model numbers, no stickers
> anywhere on it. Retouching the real photo is a separate job.

**House style.** A real US home, lived in, not a showroom: cushions slightly
dented, a blanket not perfectly folded. Mid-market, not luxury. Natural window
light. The audience is women 35+ with dogs or cats, so people in frame should read
as that, and never look posed at the lens.

**Nothing in frame may carry legible text** — no book spines, no packaging, no
signage, no car badges, no license plates. It reads as clutter in B&W and it
creates rights problems.

**Export.** JPEG, sRGB, quality ~82. Aim under 300 KB each; the hero can go to
500 KB.

## Product and gallery

### `misting-sofa.jpg` — 4:3 · min 1600×1200

Used twice, so it gets cropped two ways: full 4:3 in the "You stopped smelling it"
section, and a **1:1 centre crop** as a gallery thumbnail. Keep the subject inside
the middle square.

- **Camera** — eye level, standing, from a rear three-quarter position behind and
  to the right of the woman. 35mm equivalent, about 2.5 m back. f/2.8: the sofa
  and her hand sharp, the far side of the room falling soft.
- **Subject** — a woman around 40, shoulder-length hair loosely tied back, cream
  knit sweater, dark jeans, socks. Right arm extended, holding the unit about
  30 cm above a sofa cushion, wrist relaxed, mid-sweep. Face in three-quarter
  profile, looking at the cushion, calm and unremarkable. She is not smiling at
  the camera and does not know it is there.
- **Set** — a three-seat oatmeal fabric sofa, a throw blanket rumpled over one
  arm, one seat cushion sitting slightly askew. The edge of a coffee table blurred
  in the foreground.
- **Light** — late-afternoon window light from camera left, soft and directional.
- **The shot lives or dies on the mist**: a fine plume must be visible, catching
  the light, against the darker back of the sofa. Backlight it.
- **Framing** — woman in the left third, sofa running to the right edge, mist
  plume near centre. Do not crop her head.
- **Not in frame** — no pet, no other people, no product packaging.

### `in-hand.jpg` — 1:1 · min 1600×1600

A gallery thumbnail that also becomes the hero when selected, so it must hold up
at 105 px and at 580 px. Its only job is to answer "how big is this thing".

- **Camera** — eye level, straight on, tight. 85mm equivalent, about 1.2 m. f/4,
  so the whole unit is sharp front to back.
- **Subject** — a woman's hand holding the unit by its handle at chest height,
  forearm entering from the bottom right. Short natural nails, at most one plain
  band. The unit turned about 20° with the nozzle toward camera left so its
  silhouette reads clearly.
- **Set** — a plain, warm off-white interior wall, thrown out of focus. No props
  at all.
- **Light** — soft frontal fill, plus a gentle rim from the right. **The rim is not
  optional**: a white product on a light wall has no edge in greyscale without it.
- **Framing** — unit centred, about 65% of frame height, even margins.
- **Not in frame** — no second hand, no branding, no surface clutter.

### `dog-rug.jpg` — 1:1 · min 1600×1600

The "after" shot. Gallery thumbnail and hero. No product — the point is the
absence of a problem.

- **Camera** — low, roughly 30 cm off the floor, level with the dog's eye.
  50mm equivalent, about 2 m. f/2.5.
- **Subject** — a medium-large shedding breed, golden retriever or similar,
  curled on its side on a rug, relaxed, eyes half closed and looking softly off
  camera. Not alert, not posing.
- **Set** — a flat-weave patterned rug, a strip of hardwood at the frame edge, a
  sofa leg behind.
- **Light** — window light from behind and left, warm, throwing a halo along the
  fur.
- **Framing** — the dog fills the lower two-thirds on a diagonal; rug texture
  readable.
- **Tonal warning** — do not put a cream dog on a cream rug. Pick a rug several
  stops darker than the coat.

## How it works — three steps, 3:2 · min 1200×800 each

These sit in a row and are read as a sequence, so keep the same room, the same
light direction and the same distance feel across all three.

### `step-1.jpg` — clicking a pod into the base

- **Camera** — high, about 45° looking down, close in. 60mm macro equivalent,
  around 40 cm. f/5.6 to hold both hands sharp.
- **Subject** — two hands only. The left steadies the unit's base; the right
  thumb and index finger press and twist a refill pod into the receptacle. The
  pod is a small translucent capsule with a coloured cap, roughly 4 cm tall.
- **Set** — light wood or pale stone counter, otherwise empty.
- **Light** — soft overhead diffusion, a gentle shadow falling to the lower right.
- **Framing** — hands and unit in the left two-thirds; leave the right third as
  clean counter.
- **Not in frame** — no face, no text on the pod, nothing else on the counter.

### `step-2.jpg` — sweeping the fabric

- **Camera** — eye level, side on, perpendicular to the direction of the sweep.
  50mm equivalent, about 1.8 m. f/4. A touch of motion blur on the hand; the unit
  itself stays sharp.
- **Subject** — forearm and hand only, holding the unit about 30 cm above the seat
  cushions, travelling left to right, with a visible cone of fine mist.
- **Set** — sofa seat cushions filling the lower half of the frame.
- **Light** — from the right, raking, so the mist lights up.
- **Framing** — unit on the left third, mist trailing right into open space.
- **Not in frame** — no face, no full figure.

### `step-3.jpg` — walk away

The payoff. It should feel calm and slightly empty.

- **Camera** — standing eye level from a doorway, wide. 28mm equivalent, about
  4 m. f/5.6, everything sharp.
- **Subject** — a tidy, unoccupied living room, with a dog asleep on a dog bed or
  the end of the sofa in the mid-ground.
- **Set** — same sofa and rug as the other shots. Cushions squared, blanket
  folded, a sheer curtain at the window.
- **Light** — bright, even daylight. No hard sun patch on the floor.
- **Framing** — the room roughly symmetrical, the dog off centre to the right.
- **Not in frame** — no people, no product, no mist.

## Review photos — 4:3 · min 1200×900 each

**These must look like phone snapshots, not photography.** Slightly off-level
horizon, imperfect exposure, ordinary framing, no styling. A clean studio shot
here reads as an advertisement and kills the section's whole purpose.

> Before shipping: these are captioned "customer photo" and sit beside
> testimonials attributed to named buyers marked "verified". Generated images
> presented as real customer photographs, next to invented reviews, is the FTC
> exposure flagged in the page README. Use them as comps, and replace both the
> photos and the reviews with consented, real ones before running US traffic.

### `review-1.jpg` — dog in the back seat

- **Camera** — from the front passenger seat, twisted around, over the shoulder.
  Phone-like 26mm, about 1 m, f/1.8, handheld with a slight tilt.
- **Subject** — a golden retriever sitting up on the back seat, panting lightly,
  looking towards the camera with mild interest.
- **Set** — car interior, fabric seats, a seatbelt across frame, daylight through
  the side window slightly blown out.
- **Light** — natural and mixed, mildly imperfect. Let the window clip.
- **Not in frame** — no product, no car badge, no plate, no readable trim text.

### `review-2.jpg` — cats on the sofa

- **Camera** — standing, angled down about 20°. Phone 26mm, about 1.5 m, f/1.8.
- **Subject** — two cats on a sofa: one tabby curled asleep, one sitting upright
  looking straight at the lens.
- **Set** — an apartment sofa with a throw pulled to one side, one cushion dented.
- **Light** — an evening lamp mixing with the last window light, warm, a little
  soft.
- **Not in frame** — no product, no litter tray, no packaging.

### `review-3.jpg` — guests in the living room

- **Camera** — from a seated position at the edge of the room, eye level, candid.
  Phone 26mm, about 2.5 m, f/1.8.
- **Subject** — three or four adults mid-conversation, some seated and one
  standing, holding mugs or glasses. Nobody is looking at the camera; faces are
  partly turned or softened by movement. A dog lies at their feet.
- **Set** — the same living room as `step-3.jpg`, for continuity.
- **Light** — warm indoor lamps against the last of the daylight.
- **Not in frame** — no product, no readable text, no clearly identifiable face
  held sharp and centred.

## `order-map` — do not generate this one

8:5, in the "orders shipped" section. A generated raster is the wrong tool:

- Its pins have to be **red in the Modernist skin and blue in Cupertino**, and the
  land tone has to work on a light ground *and* on the Cupertino dark theme. One
  baked JPEG cannot do that.
- The Modernist skin greyscales all photography, which would flatten the pins into
  the land.

Build it as **inline SVG** instead: the contiguous United States as a single flat
silhouette, thin state borders, no state names, land filled from a neutral token
and roughly thirty pin dots filled from the accent token. It then follows both
skins and both themes for free, stays sharp at any size, and costs a few KB.

If it has to be a raster: 1600×1000, land in mid grey, pins as a distinctly darker
tone rather than a colour, transparent background, exported as PNG — and rename
the reference in `index.html` from `.jpg` to `.png`.
